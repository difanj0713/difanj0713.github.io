MINER

MINER: Mining Multimodal Internal Representation for Efficient Retrieval

Weien Li*, Rui Song*, Zeyu Li, Haochen Liu, Gonghao Zhang, Difan Jiao, Zhenwei Tang, Bowei He, Haolun Wu, Xue Liu, Ye Yuan  (* equal contribution)

arXiv 2605.06460 PDF

The idea

Visual document retrieval is split between two camps. Late‑interaction retrievers store hundreds of token‑level vectors per page and match them finely, which is accurate and expensive. Dense retrievers compress a page into one final‑layer embedding, which is cheap to store and serve but consistently behind on quality.

MINER argues that the gap is a readout problem rather than a capacity problem, and it is the paper in this line that needs no labels at all. It measures, layer by layer, how close each internal representation already is to the retrieval space, using centred kernel alignment plus a sample‑level alignment ratio. Those two diagnostics decide both which layers are worth extracting from and how each one should be treated: layers already coordinate‑aligned get simple reweighting, layers that hold the structure in a rotated subspace get a learned realignment first. The survivors are fused by a weighted sum back into one vector of the original dimension.

What it showed

On ViDoRe V2, MINER improved its dense backbones by up to 4.5% nDCG@5 and produced the strongest single‑vector retriever among those compared, narrowing much of the distance to late interaction. Because the output embedding keeps its shape, the index is unchanged: the same storage as dense retrieval and roughly 42× smaller than late interaction, at 1.07× the query latency.
