SPIN

Sparsifying and Integrating Internal Neurons in Large Language Models for Text Classification

Difan Jiao*, Yilun Liu*, Zhenwei Tang, Daniel Matter, Jürgen Pfeffer, Ashton Anderson  (* equal contribution)

Paper arXiv 2311.15983 Code Interactive demo

The idea

A classification head on a frozen language model normally reads one vector: the pooled hidden state of the final layer. SPIN asks what is being thrown away, and takes it back.

It fits an L1‑regularised linear probe on every layer independently, reads off which units carry the decision from the magnitude of the learned weights, keeps the smallest set whose weight mass clears a threshold, and concatenates those survivors across layers into the features the classifier actually sees. The model's weights are never touched.

What it showed

Across eight backbones spanning encoder, decoder and encoder‑decoder architectures and three datasets, SPIN improved on the final‑layer baseline in all 24 model and dataset pairs. The largest gain was Flan‑T5‑Small on EDOS, from 59.62 to 74.51 macro‑F1. On IMDb, SPIN over a frozen Flan‑T5‑XL reached 99.91% of the accuracy of a fully fine‑tuned XLNet.

Two properties from this paper carry through the whole line. The signal is sparse: at a threshold of 0.4 the method keeps roughly 3% of the units in a layer. And it is early: reading only the first 60% of the layers preserves close to 99% of the accuracy, which makes early exit free.
