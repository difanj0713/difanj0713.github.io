Findings of ACL 2024 · June 5, 2024

SPIN: Sparsifying and Integrating Internal Neurons in Large Language Models for Text Classification

We introduce SPIN, a framework that reads a frozen language model’s internal representations for text classification. It reduces classification errors by up to 71.4% compared with classifiers that use only the final layer. SPIN approaches fully fine-tuned state-of-the-art performance while training thousands of times fewer parameters; it can also skip 40% of the model’s layers at inference with little or no loss in accuracy.

Difan Jiao*, Yilun Liu*, Zhenwei Tang, Daniel Matter, Jürgen Pfeffer, Ashton Anderson

Read the paper ↗ Code ↗ Interactive demo ↗

A conventional text classifier reads the model’s final layer. SPIN sparsifies and integrates internal neurons across layers: we train a small linear probe at each layer to select task-relevant neurons, then combine their representations in a classification head. The model’s weights stay untouched; only the probes and the head are trained.

  • Performance. SPIN outperforms final-layer classifiers across all eight pretrained backbones and three benchmarks. On SST-2, it reduces classification errors by up to 71.4%. On IMDb, it reaches 96.12% accuracy, close to 96.21% for fully fine-tuned XLNet.

  • Speedup. SPIN uses less than half the estimated training compute of full fine-tuning. At inference, it can skip 40% of the model’s layers while retaining over 99% of its full-depth accuracy.1

    1 Training compute is estimated in floating-point operations (FLOPs), against one epoch of full fine-tuning. The early-exit example is GPT2-XL on IMDb. See Appendix B.2 and Table 3.

  • Interpretability. The linear probes identify which neurons, in which layers, carry information about the task. A classifier trained on whole sentences can also be applied token by token to examine how its prediction changes through a text.

Performance across model families

We evaluate SPIN on IMDb movie reviews, SST-2 sentiment classification and EDOS sexism detection. The eight pretrained backbones cover encoder, decoder and encoder-decoder architectures. In each comparison, the baseline and SPIN use the same frozen backbone and train a classifier with the dataset’s labels. The difference is which internal representations they read.

Each version of SPIN outperforms its corresponding baseline on all three benchmarks. For Flan-T5-S on EDOS, macro-F1 rises from 59.62 to 74.51, a relative improvement of about 25%.2 SPIN also improves models whose baseline performance is already high: GPT2-XL’s IMDb accuracy increases from 91.86% to 94.92%.

2 Error rate is 100% minus accuracy. For example, 90% → 95% accuracy halves the error rate, from 10% to 5%. EDOS uses macro-F1, so we report its point and relative gains instead. Table 1.

With Flan-T5-XL, SPIN reaches 96.12% accuracy on IMDb, compared with 96.21% for fully fine-tuned XLNet, the published state-of-the-art reference used in the paper. Compared with Flan-T5-XL’s own final-layer classifier, SPIN reduces the error rate from 9.50% to 3.88%, a 59.2% reduction.

Full benchmark results

Further experiments extend the comparison to larger models and more classes. On LLaMA2-13B, IMDb accuracy rises from 94.55% to 96.06%. On AG News, SPIN improves all three tested backbones using multinomial logistic regression as the probe and classification head.

Lower training cost, earlier predictions

SPIN never backpropagates through the language model. Training consists of forward passes with frozen weights to record the representations, then fitting the linear probes and classification head on those recordings. For GPT2-XL, that means training about one million parameters instead of 1.6 billion.

The forward passes dominate SPIN’s training cost. It uses only slightly more compute than training a final-layer classifier, and less than half the estimated operations of a single epoch of full fine-tuning. The probes can be fitted independently on the recorded representations.

At inference, SPIN can classify text using only the layers up to a chosen exit point. With 60% of its layers, GPT2-XL reaches 94.73% IMDb accuracy, compared with 94.92% at full depth. Flan-T5 and Flan-T5-XL reach their full-depth accuracy at 60%. The useful exit point depends on the backbone: DistilBERT loses more than two points at the same depth.

Further gains after fine-tuning

SPIN needs only access to a model’s internal representations, so it can also be applied after the model has been fine-tuned for a task. On publicly available fine-tuned DistilBERT, RoBERTa and GPT2 checkpoints, SPIN improves on the model’s own classifier in every available comparison.

The gains here are smaller, often under one point, because the fine-tuned model has already adapted to the task. Five-fold cross-validation on IMDb confirms that all three improvements are significant at p < 0.05. On a LoRA-fine-tuned DistilBERT, SPIN raises IMDb accuracy from 87.71% to 90.58%.

What the selected neurons reveal

SPIN can be inspected at two levels: the individual neurons selected by the probes, and the classifier built from them. In this GPT2-XL example, later-layer neurons distinguish positive and negative reviews more clearly, while combining neurons across layers separates the classes more cleanly than any individual neuron shown.

Why look beyond the final layer?

Interpretability research has repeatedly found task-relevant information inside language models. Radford et al. (2017) identified a single neuron that classifies sentiment;4 later work used selected transformer neurons as classifiers. SPIN builds on these observations to make a general classification method that works across model families.

4 The neuron emerged through unsupervised language-model training. Fixing its value to be positive or negative also changed the sentiment of the text the model generated. Radford et al. (2017).

A final-layer classifier uses a single representation of the text. SPIN gives the classifier access to selected features from several depths, combining simpler features in lower layers with more abstract ones higher up. Filtering matters: irrelevant neurons add noise that dilutes the features useful for the task.

Both parts of SPIN contribute. On GPT2-XL and IMDb, selecting one neuron per layer with the probes gives 93.71% accuracy, compared with 69.90% for random selection.5 Combining layers also outperforms the best individual layer at every exit depth we tested.

5 Sparsification selects which features the classifier reads. The backbone still computes the intervening layers; early exit saves work by stopping before the final layer. Section 2.2.

Neuron selection and integration in more detail

We pool each layer’s hidden states or feed-forward activations over tokens to obtain a fixed-size vector. An L1-regularized logistic regression then predicts the task label from that layer’s representations. The penalty pushes many weights to zero; we normalize the remaining weight magnitudes and keep the smallest set whose cumulative weight reaches a chosen threshold.

At a threshold of 0.4, SPIN keeps about 3% of the neurons in each layer in this analysis. The selected representations are concatenated into one feature vector, and a linear classification head is trained on it.

The approach requires access to internal representations, so it cannot be used through a text-only API. We kept the classification head linear to match prior work and make its inputs inspectable; a more sophisticated head could improve accuracy. The paper includes the mathematical formulation, hyperparameter settings and remaining experiments.

For the tasks we studied, intermediate representations provide useful features even when the backbone’s weights stay fixed. SPIN shows how selecting and combining those features can improve classification, reduce adaptation cost and make predictions before the final layer.
