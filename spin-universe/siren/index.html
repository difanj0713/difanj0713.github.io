SIREN

LLM Safety From Within: Detecting Harmful Content with Internal Representations

Difan Jiao, Yilun Liu, Ye Yuan, Zhenwei Tang, Linfeng Du, Haolun Wu, Ashton Anderson

Paper arXiv 2604.18519 Code Models pip install llm-siren

The idea

A guard model is the standard way to keep harmful content out of a deployed system: a separate, safety‑tuned model reads a prompt or a response and generates a verdict. That verdict is decoded from the final layer, which puts the entire safety judgment through the narrowest part of the network.

SIREN takes the SPIN recipe to that setting. It locates safety neurons layer by layer, weights each layer by how well its own probe did on validation data, and trains a small classifier on the aggregate. Nothing in the underlying model changes.

What it showed

The comparison is deliberately tight: SIREN is trained on the internal states of exactly the backbone each guard was fine‑tuned from, so the only thing that differs is how the verdict is read. Across seven safety benchmarks, SIREN came out ahead on all four backbone pairs, from 0.6B to 8B. On Qwen3‑4B it reached 86.7 average F1 against Qwen3Guard‑4B's 83.4; on Llama‑3.2‑1B, 85.7 against LlamaGuard3‑1B's 70.7.

It did that with 14M trainable parameters against roughly 4B, and inference is a single forward pass rather than autoregressive decoding. Two properties came for free. It transfers to a benchmark it never saw, built from reasoning traces. And although it was only ever trained on complete sequences, applying the same fitted readout to growing prefixes turns it into a streaming detector that catches more harmful generations, at every latency position, than a guard purpose‑built for streaming with token‑level supervision.
